About

The long way to snow.

It started with a film, and a race on television.

She grew up on her family's coffee farm at Perur, near Napoklu in Kodagu, where it has never snowed and where neither of her parents had ever seen snow either. In 2013 she watched a Bollywood film with a trekking sequence in it and went looking for the mountains it was set in. That took her to Darjeeling, to a mountaineering qualification, and to a job instructing in Kashmir, which is where she first put on skis.

Cross-country came later, and secondhand. Watching the 2018 Pyeongchang Games she saw the Norwegian Marit Bjørgen race, and decided that was the event she wanted. She left the instructing job to train full time. Her father, Thekkada Nanjunda, who has spent his life on the coffee farm, told her that if it made her happy she should do it.

The sport

The sport splits two ways, by technique and by distance. She races every combination.

India has no permanent snow in her discipline, so her year follows it across both hemispheres: Kashmir and Europe from December to March, conditioning on the coast through the Indian summer, then South America or New Zealand in August and September.

Classic
Skis run inside two grooves cut into the snow. Her sprint at Harbin was a classical race.
Freestyle
A skating stride on a wide groomed piste, no grooves. Her 5 km at Harbin was freestyle, in 18:10.
Sprint
About 1.3 to 1.5 km, run as knockout heats. She reached the quarter-final at Harbin in 2025.
Distance
10 or 15 km, alone against the clock. Bronze at both distances at Gulmarg in 2026.

Speaking

She has spent ten years explaining a sport nobody around her had heard of, to selectors, to sponsors and to customs officers. The talks come out of that.
